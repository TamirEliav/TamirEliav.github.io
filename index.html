About

I'm a postdoctoral researcher in the Vallentin lab at the Max Planck Institute for Biological Intelligence in Seewiesen, Germany, where I study the neural circuits that underlie vocal communication.

I completed my PhD and MSc with Prof. Nachum Ulanovsky at the Weizmann Institute of Science, studying how the hippocampus represents space and memory in freely flying bats navigating very large environments. Before neuroscience, I earned a BSc in pure mathematics from Bar-Ilan University and spent several years working on embedded software and algorithm R&D. I'm drawn to questions at the intersection of neural computation, naturalistic behavior, and the mathematics of how the brain encodes information.

Research

  • Systems neuroscience
  • Hippocampus & spatial memory
  • Neural coding
  • Navigation
  • Vocal communication
  • In vivo electrophysiology

My research asks how the brain represents complex, naturalistic behavior at the level of neural circuits. I combine large-scale electrophysiology with quantitative analysis and theory to understand how populations of neurons encode information.

Neural circuits for vocal communication

Current Investigating how neural circuits support vocal communication and vocal learning — how the brain produces, controls, and coordinates communication signals.

Hippocampal coding of very large spaces

How the hippocampus represents space across scales in freely flying bats, revealing multiscale place codes and nonoscillatory phase coding in natural, large-scale environments.

Memory replay in natural environments

Characterizing how the hippocampus replays experience in environments far larger than the classic laboratory setting, including fragmented replay of very large spaces.
